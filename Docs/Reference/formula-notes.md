# Formula notes

Where the maths in `calculationService.js` and `recommendationService.js` is
wrong, what to replace it with, and what the numbers say. Everything here is
measured on the eight simulated cooks in `tools/sim`; regenerate with
`npm run sim` and the projection experiment described at the end.

> **Scope: 2–5 hour cooks.** No overnight cooks are planned, so every figure
> below is scored on the seven short cooks in the deck and the 12-hour pork
> shoulder is excluded. That exclusion changes two conclusions materially — see
> [What the scope change revised](#what-the-scope-change-revised).

---

## 1. The projection is a straight line through a curve

`calculateHeatingRate` fits a least-squares line to the last N readings, and
`predictTimeToTarget` extrapolates it:

```
minutesRemaining = (targetTemp - currentTemp) / rate * 60
```

A roast is not linear. Fitting Newton's law of cooling to the two intervals of
the real export gives `k = 0.00193 /min` and then `k = 0.00378 /min` — the rate
constant **doubles**. Early in a cook the core is still waiting for the thermal
wave to arrive, so it *accelerates*; a line fitted there extrapolates to a
finishing time far too late.

### What it costs

Projection error against the true remaining time, at every reading of the seven
short cooks:

| projection | MAE | worst | MAE at first advice | worst at first advice |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **line** (shipped) | 17.5 min | 115 min | **33.1 min** | **115 min** |
| single-node exponential | 40.2 min | 219 min | 79.4 min | 219 min |
| **two-stage lag, one constant** | **3.0 min** | 7 min | **3.6 min** | **7 min** |

The first-advice column is the one that matters: it is the moment the app opens
its mouth, and it is wrong by half an hour on average and by nearly two hours at
worst. Seven of eight cooks open with *"running late, consider raising the
oven"*; seven of eight then finish **early**.

### The replacement

Two first-order lags in series, one fitted constant:

```
dTsurface/dt = k * (Toven - Tsurface)
dTcore/dt    = k * (Tsurface - Tcore)
```

Step response — this is the whole point — is

```
Tcore(t) = Toven - (Toven - T0) * (1 + k*t) * e^(-k*t)
```

which starts at **zero slope**, accelerates, then decays. That is the shape the
real readings have. A line cannot produce it and a single exponential produces
the decay without the acceleration, which is why the middle row of the table
above is *worse than shipping today*: it has the wrong curvature exactly where
the error is largest. **Do not reach for the obvious "use an exponential
approach" fix.**

The app already holds everything the fit needs — the readings and the oven event
history. Fit `k` by least squares over the readings, integrating through the
recorded set-point changes, with `Tsurface(0) = Tcore(0)` (true: the whole roast
leaves the fridge cold). Then integrate forward to target.

`tools/sim/meatModel.js` is a working implementation of the forward integration
and `tools/sim/calibrate.js` of the fit; between them they are about 40 lines of
the arithmetic that would need to move into `calculationService.js`.

**On `kOven` vs `kCore`.** The harness carries them separately and the fit lands
on `kOven == kCore == 0.010991 /min`, with the ridge check in `calibrate.js`
showing error rising steeply either side of equality (SSE 0.43 at ratio 1, 2.13
at 2, 22.9 at 4). One constant is enough for the app.

**Honest caveat.** The 3.0 min figure is scored against a world generated by a
two-stage model, so treat it as a ceiling rather than a forecast. The comparison
against the exponential arm does not depend on that, and neither does the
diagnosis: the real export's own data selected this shape.

### The cheap version, if the rewrite is too big

The acceleration signal is already computed and thrown away. `currentRate`
(windowed) and `averageRate` (whole session) diverge sharply while the cook
accelerates, and the sign of the divergence says which phase you are in. Refusing
to advise — or damping the suggestion — while `currentRate > averageRate * ~1.3`
is a few lines.

Measured proxy for this (deferring first advice to 4 readings / 60 min span): dial
moves **12 → 2**, reversals **4 → 0**. But schedule accuracy barely moves (46 →
45 min) and silence doubles (485 → 1100 min across the seven cooks). So the guard
prevents the app from doing harm; it does not make it useful. It is a stopgap on
the way to the projection change, not a substitute.

## 2. R² is measuring the wrong thing

`assessConfidence` grades on the R² of the linear fit:

```js
if (r2 < 0.7)  -> low,    "Temperature readings are fluctuating"
if (r2 < 0.9)  -> medium
r2 >= 0.9 && readings >= 4 && span >= 30 -> high
```

R² says how well a **line** fits the points. It does not say whether a line is
the right model. Three readings on a smooth accelerating curve are very nearly
collinear, so R² is *high* — and the extrapolation is still 33 minutes out. The
app reports `medium` and then `high` confidence over exactly the stretch where the
projection is at its worst.

Worse, the failure mode when R² does drop is mislabelled: real curvature is
reported to the user as *"Temperature readings are fluctuating"*, and at
`r2 < 0.7` it becomes the `unstable_rate` blocker and stops advice altogether.
Noise and curvature are different problems and only one of them is the user's.

**Replacement:** grade on the residual of the *curved* fit. Once change 1 lands,
the fit's own RMS residual against the readings is a direct, meaningful measure —
large residual means the readings genuinely disagree with any smooth heating
curve, which is what "fluctuating" should mean. Until then, R² should not be
allowed to produce `high`.

Also worth noting: `readingCount >= 4` for high confidence means, at the ~45 min
cadence real cooks use, that a 2.5 hour cook reaches `high` only near the end —
there are only four or five readings in the whole cook.

## 3. `calculateOvenOffDuration` is not the physics it claims to be

```js
const pauseFactor = scheduleVarianceMinutes / predictedMinutesToTarget;
const suggestedPause = Math.round(predictedMinutesToTarget * pauseFactor * 0.5);
```

`predictedMinutesToTarget` cancels. The existing comment says so, to its credit,
but the function is introduced as a "physics-based approach" and it reduces to

```
pause = clamp(scheduleVariance * 0.5, 5, 45)
```

Half of how early you are, capped at 45 minutes. That is a reasonable heuristic —
but it is a heuristic, and the 45-minute cap means a cook running 110 minutes
early (scenario 04) gets a 45-minute pause and then has to be told again. Either
drop the pretence and write the one-liner, or use the model: with a fitted `k` and
an oven cooling constant you can solve for the pause length that lands on the
serve time, which is the same arithmetic as change 1 run backwards.

## 4. `recommendationMaxStepF` is applied before the dial snap

A genuine defect rather than a modelling choice, and fixed alongside this note:

```
base 95 °C (203 °F), running >30 min late
  changeAmount = min(25, 10 * 2.5) = 25 °F      <- capped here
  203 + 25 = 228 °F = 108.9 °C
  snapToDial('nearest') -> 110 °C = 230 °F      <- rounds up, past the cap
  changeAmount = 230 - 203 = 27 °F              <- recomputed, never re-checked
```

Celsius sessions only, because the 5 °C grid is coarser than the 25 °F cap. The
cap has to be re-applied after snapping.

## What the scope change revised

Dropping overnight cooks changed two of my earlier conclusions. Both corrections
are in this direction: things I had called dangerous are safe at 2–5 hours.

**The stale-oven gate is safe to demote on its own.** Across all eight cooks,
removing `ovenTempStaleMinutes` as a blocker made things worse — the 12-hour
shoulder oscillated through eight dial moves and never finished. Across the seven
short cooks it is **free**: schedule miss identical (46 → 46 min), reversals
identical (4 → 4), and silence drops from 485 to 310 minutes. The oscillation
risk was entirely the overnight cook. A steady dial is not missing data, and
`StatusCards.vue:136` already renders an "out of date" chip, so the blocker is
redundant with a warning that exists.

**The stall no longer matters.** A 2–5 hour cook to 125–140 °F never enters the
150–165 °F stall band, so the app's misdiagnosis of a stall as *"Check thermometer
placement"* is out of scope. Leave `RATE_TOO_LOW` alone: at these lengths a
near-zero rate really is most likely a misplaced probe or an oven that went off,
and the message is good advice. Worth revisiting only if a shoulder or brisket
ever gets cooked.

The 12-hour shoulder stays in the deck as an out-of-scope stress case — it is
where the control loop's instability shows up most clearly — but it should not
drive any decision. Score changes on the seven short cooks.

## What no formula change will fix

Overshoot. Across every configuration tried — stale gate off, deferred advice,
wider dead band, smaller steps, faster settling, and combinations — mean
overshoot stayed between **14.6 and 15.6 °F** and the worst between **29.9 and
31.0 °F**. Nothing moved it, because nothing in the app ever asks for a reading.

At 40 °F/h near the end of a cook, a 45-minute reading gap is 30 °F of core
temperature. The app holds `predictedTargetTime` the whole way and never uses it
to say *"take a reading at 8:19"*. In the replay of the real cook, a roast
targeted at 54.4 °C is served at **71.6 °C**.

This is the change that decides whether a medium-rare order arrives medium-rare,
and it is not in the formulas at all — it is a missing prompt. Related: `at-target`
fires on `latestReading >= targetTemp` and reads identically at +0.1 °F and
+31 °F over, so the app cannot even tell you after the fact.

## Rejected — measured, made things worse or did nothing

| change | result on the seven short cooks |
|---|---|
| single-node exponential projection | MAE 40.2 min vs 17.5 shipped — **worse** |
| `onTrackThresholdMinutes` 10 → 25 | schedule miss 46 → 46 min — **no effect** |
| `recommendationStepF/Max` 10/25 → 5/10 | miss 46 → 74 min, overshoot 15.0 → 14.2 °F, still 4 reversals — **worse** |
| defer first advice, alone | miss 46 → 45 min, silence 485 → 1100 min — **inert** |

## Reproducing the numbers

```bash
npm run sim                                              # baseline
SIM_SETTINGS='{"ovenTempStaleMinutes":100000}' npm run sim   # a candidate change
```

`tools/sim/artifacts/summary.json` carries overshoot, schedule miss, silence,
dial moves and reversals per scenario. The deck is deterministic, so any movement
is attributable to the change.

The projection comparison is not committed — it is a throwaway script that
replays each scenario's model, generates readings at that scenario's cadence, and
scores `calculateHeatingRate` + `predictTimeToTarget` against a single-node fit,
a two-stage fit, and the model's own forward integration to target. Rebuild it
from `tools/sim/meatModel.js` plus the three arms described in section 1.

See `tools/sim/README.md#limits` before trusting any of this too far — chiefly
that the model is calibrated on 89 minutes of one cook, with nothing above 33 °C
core, and the endgame is both the least constrained part of the model and the part
every finding here turns on.
